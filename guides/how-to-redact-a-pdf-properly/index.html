How to redact a PDF properly (and why black boxes are not enough)

By Manikant Yadav, maker of SmallPDF.us · Updated 2026-10-13

Every year, documents are published with "blacked out" names that anyone can copy out in seconds. The reason: covering text with a black shape hides it visually but leaves the text in the file.

Why a black box is not redaction

In a PDF, the text and the box are separate layers. The box sits on top, the text is still underneath. Select-all and copy, or open the file in a text extractor, and the "hidden" words appear.

What real redaction does

Real redaction removes the underlying content. One reliable method is to turn the affected page into an image with the sensitive area permanently blacked out. Nothing is left underneath because the page is no longer text.

Redact PDF works this way: you draw boxes over what must disappear, and the pages are rendered with those areas burned in. The trade-off is that the result is image-based, so the text on those pages can no longer be selected.

Steps

  1. Open Redact PDF and upload the document.
  2. Draw a box over every piece of sensitive information: names, account numbers, signatures, faces, addresses.
  3. Apply the redaction and download the new file.
  4. Test it. Open the result, press select-all and copy into a text editor. The redacted parts must not show up. Also search for a redacted word.

Other places data can hide

  • Metadata: author name, title and editing software.
  • Other pages and attachments: the same name may appear again on a later page.
  • Bookmarks and comments: they can contain the text you removed from the page.
Keep the original. Redaction is permanent. Save an untouched copy somewhere safe and only share the redacted version.

To restrict who can open a file instead of removing content, use Protect PDF to add a password. If you forget a password you set, Unlock PDF can remove it when you know the password.
