How to compress a PDF without losing quality

By Manikant Yadav, maker of SmallPDF.us · Updated 2026-10-13

A PDF that is too big to email or upload is one of the most common office annoyances. The good news is that most PDFs can be made much smaller without anyone noticing a difference. This guide explains where the size comes from and how to shrink it safely.

Why PDFs get large

  • Images. Photos, scans and screenshots are usually 90% or more of the file size. A PDF made of scanned pages is really a stack of pictures.
  • Embedded fonts. Every font used in the document can be stored inside the file, sometimes in full instead of only the letters you use.
  • Leftover data. Editing history, thumbnails, unused objects and metadata add weight that nobody ever sees.

What compression changes

A good compressor does three things: it re-encodes images at a lower resolution or quality, it removes duplicated or unused data, and it re-packs the internal streams more efficiently. Text stays text, so it remains sharp and searchable. Only images lose some detail.

Steps

  1. Open Compress PDF and drop your file on the page.
  2. Pick a level. A balanced level is right for most documents; choose a stronger level only when you must hit a strict size limit.
  3. Download the result and open it. Check the pages with photos or small print.
  4. If it is still too large, compress again with a stronger level, or split the document into parts with Split PDF.
Tip: if the PDF has fillable form fields, keep a copy of the original. Forms need their fields intact, so only lossless savings are safe for them.

Common size limits

Many email services cap attachments at around 20-25 MB, and many online application forms accept much less (often 1-5 MB). Check the limit before you compress so you know what to aim for, and leave a little margin.

If the file will not get smaller

  • It may already be compressed. Files exported from a modern tool often have little left to remove.
  • Scanned pages at a very high resolution are the hardest case. Re-scan at 150-200 DPI in black and white for text documents.
  • Convert pages to images only as a last resort, because you lose searchable text.
