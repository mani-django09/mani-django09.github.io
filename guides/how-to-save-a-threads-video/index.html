How to save a video from a public Threads post (and make it usable)

By Manikant Yadav, maker of SmallPDF.us and Threads Downloader · Updated 2026-10-13

Threads has no obvious “save video” button, so people end up screen-recording, which gives a blurry copy with notifications in the corner. A better way is to save the actual video file. This guide walks through it from start to finish, including the parts most tutorials skip: finding the file afterwards, checking it is complete, and making it small enough to send.

Before you start: what can and cannot be saved

  • Public posts only. If you can open the post in a private or incognito browser window without logging in, it can be saved. If the account is private, it cannot, and it should not be.
  • The post must actually contain a video. A link preview to a YouTube video or a news article is not a video attached to the post.
  • It is not yours unless you made it. Keeping a copy for yourself is one thing. Reposting someone else's clip needs their permission. More on that at the end.

Step 1: copy the link to the post

In the Threads app, tap the share arrow under the post and choose Copy link. On a computer, copy the address from the post's page. Three kinds of links work:

  • The full address, such as threads.net/@name/post/ABC123
  • The short threads.net/t/ABC123 form
  • The share links the mobile app creates

Copy the link from the post itself, not from someone's profile page, which points to the account and not to one video.

Step 2: paste it into a downloader

Open a Threads video downloader in your normal browser (Safari, Chrome, Samsung Internet), paste the link and press the button. You should see a preview of the clip with its file size before anything is saved. If the preview is the wrong video, you pasted the wrong link; go back and copy it again.

If you are on a phone and the page opened inside another app, copy the address into a real browser first. Built-in app browsers often block downloads.

Step 3: find the file

DeviceWhere it goesGetting it into your camera roll
iPhone / iPadFiles app, in Downloads (iCloud Drive or On My iPhone)Open the video in Files, tap Share, choose Save Video
AndroidDownloads folder, via the Files or My Files appUsually appears in the Gallery on its own; otherwise move it from Files
Windows / MacYour Downloads folderDrag it wherever you keep videos

On iPhone you can choose where Safari saves files under Settings, Safari, Downloads. Picking On My iPhone keeps big videos off your iCloud storage.

Step 4: check the file is complete

An interrupted download can leave a file that plays for a few seconds and stops. A thirty-second check avoids finding that out later:

  1. Play the first and last few seconds, with sound on.
  2. Compare its size with the size shown in the preview. A file far smaller than expected was cut short.
  3. On a computer, open the file's properties and make sure the duration looks right.

If it is incomplete, download it again, ideally on Wi-Fi and with the screen on until it finishes.

Step 5: make it small enough to send

Most short clips are a few megabytes, but longer ones can be too large for email or a chat app. Your options, easiest first:

  • Send a link instead of the file. Upload it to a cloud drive and share the link. The video keeps its quality.
  • Send it as a file, not a video. Chat apps compress anything sent as a normal video. Sending it as a document skips that.
  • Trim it. The built-in editors in Photos (iPhone) and Gallery or Google Photos (Android) can cut it down to the part you need.
  • Re-encode it smaller. If you are comfortable with a command line, ffmpeg can shrink a clip a lot:

ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 -vf scale=-2:480 -crf 28 clip-small.mp4

That reduces the picture to 480 pixels high and compresses it harder. It is fine for sharing a quick reaction; do not use it for a copy you want to keep as an archive, because re-encoding always loses some quality.

A note on permission and credit

Saving a video for yourself, to watch offline or to keep something useful, is the ordinary case. Anything beyond that, such as posting it on your own account, using it in a video or an advert, or putting it in a course, needs the creator's say-so. Ask in a reply or message; most people agree, and you get a better file as a bonus. Credit the creator, but do not treat credit as a substitute for permission.
